Duperfit Balmer-Line Audit

An independent check of the ten figures of Hα equivalent widths and Hα/Hβ ratios, and the corrected measurements on 1,648 public spectra of the same 99 supernovae from the Open Supernova Catalog and WISeREP, with the trends they show.

The strip spans 4000–7000 Å, the range of the single polynomial continuum in the student-style method. Shaded boxes are the ±10,000 km/s windows this audit integrates over (4699–5023 Å and 6344–6782 Å), with continuum sidebands just outside them.

Verdict

What holds up and what does not

  • Validated

    Hα EWs of the interacting classes

    Where the measurement is well-defined, the student's values agree with ours to a median of 0.08 dex for SNe IIn (116 points), 0.10 dex for SLSN-II and 0.13 dex for SNe Ia-CSM. The public spectra show the same IIn trend: |EW| grows from about 100 Å near maximum to several hundred Å after 60 d.

  • Not reliable

    SN II and SN IIb EWs

    The 50 positive EWs (11 SN II, 34 IIb) and the sign changes come from P-Cygni absorption and where the continuum is placed. In nebular IIb spectra the Hα window is dominated by [O I] 6300,6364. Only 14 of the 110 IIb points are well-defined, and SN II |EW| are a median 1.9× smaller than ours.

  • Not valid

    Most Hα/Hβ values are not Balmer decrements

    Only 77 of 540 plotted ratios come from spectra where both lines are significant, emission-dominated and unblended. The 170 negative ratios and those beyond ±30 occur when the integrated "Hβ flux" is near zero; moving the window by 10 Å flips their sign. Where a decrement is defined, IIn values agree to 0.14 dex.

  • Data defect

    One corrupted template makes the extreme points

    The SN 1999br "+41 d" template drops to −240,000 across Hα (median flux ≈ 1). It produced the −106 EW and +104 ratio. SN 1987A "+44 d" and "+70 d" are one spectrum, and two SN 1998S templates have wavelengths in µm.

  • Phase axis

    Three phase conventions on one axis

    SN II phases count from discovery (SN 1987A's "day 1" is ~78 d before maximum), most IIn, IIb and SLSN phases from maximum, several IIn from discovery, and SN 1995N from a spurious catalogue date: its "+51 d" spectrum was taken 411 d after discovery.

  • Templates faithful

    The template bank matches the public data

    For 314 templates identified with their source spectrum, EW agrees to 0.01 dex and Hα/Hβ to 0.03 dex; the relative Hβ/Hα calibration differs by more than 0.1 dex in only 2 % of 275 pairs. The problems come from the measurement, not the templates.

Presentation issues on top: the "SLSN-II" figures also contain the six SLSN-IIn objects (2006gy and five PTF10 SNe); OGLE-2013-SN-016 and -019 share one legend entry; the default symlog axis is linear between −2 and +2, which makes near-zero values look like a separate population; there are no units, uncertainties or extinction corrections.

Why the ratios fail

A ratio of two signed integrals

F(Hα)/F(Hβ) is a Balmer decrement only when both integrals are emission fluxes. In photospheric SN II and IIb spectra, and in SNe Ia-CSM, the Hβ window holds a P-Cygni profile blended with Fe II 4924, 5018 or the underlying SN Ia spectrum. Its continuum-subtracted integral scatters around zero, so the ratio takes any sign and size. With the student-style continuum, moving the window edge by 10 Å swings the SN II and Ia-CSM ratios between +104 and −104, while a clean IIn line (SN 2010jl) stays at 4–7.

Public spectra

What the original data support

The corrected measurements on all public spectra, with the trends for each class, are in the Results tab. In short: the Hα |EW| of SNe IIn grows from about 100 Å near maximum to about 270 Å at 60–150 d, and their Balmer decrements (median 4.4) steepen with time; SNe Ia-CSM show no measurable Hβ emission; SNe II give decrements only within a week of maximum (median 2.2).

Recommendations

For the next version of the analysis

  • Clean the inputs. Drop or repair the defective templates above and de-duplicate before counting epochs.
  • Measure decrements only where they exist. Use emission-dominated lines; fit and remove blends (He I 4922/5016, [O III] 4959/5007, Fe II 4924/5018) or fit narrow, intermediate and broad components; require both fluxes to be significant; never report a negative ratio. State the extinction treatment: E(B−V) = 0.3 changes Hα/Hβ by about 35 %.
  • Split P-Cygni lines. For SNe II and IIb, report absorption and emission pseudo-EWs (a/e) instead of a net EW, and exclude nebular spectra where [O I] enters the Hα window.
  • One phase reference. Put every object on explosion or maximum using the offsets in results/template_phase_offsets.csv.
  • Plot what is measured. Log axes for positive quantities, uncertainties, units; SLSN-II and SLSN-IIn kept apart; legend names parsed correctly.
  • Test the choices. Check every value's sensitivity to the window and continuum before reading trends into it. The Monte-Carlo in bv/measure.py does this.
Limits

What this audit cannot settle

  • The student's code was not available. His values were read from the JPEG figures (axis calibration better than 0.5 px) and his exact windows could not be recovered: no variant of our reconstruction reproduces his SN II and IIb numbers.
  • The quality checks are deliberately conservative; component fitting could recover some flagged spectra. Host extinction is not corrected anywhere.
  • Only 302 of the 660 templates within −20…150 d (46 %; SLSN-II 17 %) have a public counterpart above r = 0.95. SN 2019gsp has no redshift on WISeREP and is not measured.

Results · observed sample

Hα strength and Balmer decrements across supernova classes

Corrected measurements on every public spectrum of the 99 objects (OSC and WISeREP; 1,035 usable spectra between −20 and +150 d), with the method of this audit. Only reliable values appear: clean Hα emission equivalent widths and valid Balmer decrements. Spectra of one object from the same night are merged into one value, and population statistics count objects, not spectra: medians over objects in phase bins, and 95 % intervals from resampling objects. Phases are days from maximum light, or from discovery where the catalogue maximum is implausible (6 SNe IIn, 3 SNe Ia-CSM, and one SN II, IIb and SLSN-II).

  • Hα strengthens with time only in the interacting classes. From within ±20 d of maximum to 60–150 d, the median |EW| grows ×2.7 in SNe IIn (100 → 273 Å), ×3.2 in SLSN-II (27 → 89 Å) and ×2.6 in SNe Ia-CSM (101 → 258 Å). In normal SNe II it stays flat (165 → 136 Å).

  • Balmer decrements separate the classes. SNe IIn: median 4.4, two thirds of nights above Case B, steepening with time. SNe Ia-CSM: Hβ is not detected, and Hα/Hβ exceeds 10 on 81 % of nights against the local continuum. SNe II: 2.2, and only within a week of maximum.

Interacting

SNe IIn

37 objects with clean Hα EWs on 161 nights · 15 objects with Balmer decrements on 52 nights

  • Hα grows relative to the continuum. Median |EW| over objects: ≈ 80 Å before maximum, ≈ 100 Å at 0–20 d, ≈ 170 Å at 40–70 d, ≈ 270 Å at 70–100 d and ≈ 610 Å at 100–150 d. Within single objects, 17 of 22 rise (median +0.39 dex per 100 d, about ×2.4); pooled Spearman ρ = +0.51 (95 % interval 0.23 to 0.69). Dropping the six objects whose phases count from discovery leaves ρ = +0.44.
  • Early Hα strength differs widely between objects. Near maximum |EW| spans about 20–1000 Å. Some objects are Hα-dominated from the start (1997eg ≈ 1000 Å, 2015da ≈ 340 Å, 2010jl ≈ 210 Å); others start weak and climb as the continuum fades (1998S 34 → 1340 Å, 2008fq 19 → 220 Å, 2000eo 35 → 130 Å).
  • Balmer decrements are mostly steeper than Case B. Median 4.4 (interquartile 2.6–5.9, range 1.1–16); 67 % of nights lie above 2.86. Milky Way reddening changes them by at most 8 % (median 2 %). Host extinction is not corrected and would lower them.
  • Decrements steepen after maximum. About 2.7 within ±20 d of maximum (12 objects), 4.4 after 40 d (7 objects) and 6–8 beyond 70 d; ρ(phase) = +0.49 (0.13 to 0.75). Five of the six objects with three or more epochs steepen: 2015da 4.7 → 13.5, 1997eg 5.2 → 8.3, 2010jl 2.9 → 4.3, 2006aa 2.2 → 2.6 and LSQ12hxg 1.1 → 1.3; 1999eb stays near 2.7.
  • Flat decrements are rare. Only LSQ12hxg stays below 2 (1.1–1.3), the regime expected where Hα is optically thick in very dense gas (Drake & Ulrich 1980). Steep and steepening decrements point to collisional excitation in dense gas, growing internal extinction, or both; dust formation has been reported in SN 2010jl (Gall et al. 2014).
  • Decrement against Hα strength is only suggestive. Nights with larger |EW| have steeper decrements (ρ = +0.59), but most of this is the shared trend with phase: at fixed phase the correlation is +0.43 (−0.27 to 0.86), and between objects it is +0.46 (p = 0.09, 15 objects).

Interacting

SNe Ia-CSM

6 objects with clean Hα EWs on 36 nights · no valid Balmer decrement; lower limits on 32 nights

  • The tightest growth of Hα with time. Median |EW| is about 100 Å within ±20 d (5 objects) and 260 Å at 60–150 d (4 objects); ρ(phase) = +0.80 (0.40 to 0.88), the strongest correlation of any class. All four objects with three or more epochs rise: 2011km 53 → 247 Å, 2012ca 192 → 497 Å, 2017eby 102 → 162 Å and 1999E 234 → 288 Å.
  • Hβ is not detected as an emission line. In none of the 54 spectra covering both lines is Hβ significant net emission, and in 21 it is net absorption. Against the local continuum, Hα/Hβ exceeds 10 on 81 % of nights and Case B on all of them (median lower limit about 20): decrements far steeper than in SNe IIn.
  • The limits depend on the continuum. Hβ sits on the Fe-dominated SN Ia spectrum, so a value needs that spectrum modelled and removed. Sharma et al. (2023) report decrements of about 7 with a different method.

SNe II

11 objects with clean Hα EWs on 114 nights · Balmer decrements for 3 objects within a week of maximum

  • No growth of Hα relative to the continuum. Median |EW| stays at about 110–180 Å from −20 to +150 d; ρ(phase) = −0.05 (−0.24 to 0.18). Per-object slopes scatter around zero (median +0.09 dex per 100 d); the three that rise (1987A, 2004dj, 2009N) are sampled as their photospheric phase ends.
  • Flat decrements near maximum only. Eleven nights of 1999em, 2002gd and 2005cs within a week of maximum: median 2.2 (interquartile 1.7–2.8), 27 % above Case B. These lines form near the photosphere, so this is not a recombination value; later Hβ turns into P-Cygni absorption blended with Fe II.
  • The contrast with interacting events. The ×2.6–3.2 growth of |EW| seen in all three interacting classes is absent here.

How to read these numbers

Caveats

  • Samples are small and heterogeneous: what the archives happen to hold, with uneven phase coverage. Treat the 95 % intervals, which resample objects, as the honest uncertainty, not the per-spectrum errors.
  • Decrements are not corrected for host extinction; Milky Way reddening is at most an 8 % effect here.
  • Values are full-line integrals over ±10,000 km/s. Narrow and intermediate CSM components are not separated, so decrements of individual components can differ.
  • Phases from discovery (marked in the data tables) are offset from maximum by an unknown amount, usually a few weeks or less.
